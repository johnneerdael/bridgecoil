Beta privacy.

Bridgecoil controls a selected Android device directly from your controller. This page describes data handling in the Bridgecoil controller beta.

Information on your controller

Bridgecoil keeps connection preferences, remembered device details, app appearance and saved command choices on the controller. It creates an ADB host identity so an authorized TV can recognize it on later connections.

Device information, package details, commands and operation results are used to show the selected target and its state. Operation history is stored locally. Selected APKs and transfers may use private staging or cache files. Files and captures you choose to save are written to the destination selected through the operating system.

The controller has no Bridgecoil account system, advertising or analytics SDK. Its device-management path does not use a developer-operated cloud relay.

Connections to your Android device

After authorization, Bridgecoil sends commands and selected files directly to the Android device on the local network and receives its responses, files, captures and logs. Discovery browses local ADB service advertisements.

Modern wireless ADB supports TLS. An optional legacy connection is unencrypted and requires explicit consent. Local Network permission supports discovery and connection; it does not grant the developer access to your network.

External package information and downloads

Bridgecoil can request community package descriptions and ratings, helper release information and helper downloads from GitHub services. These providers receive ordinary network-request information, such as the requesting IP address and request headers, under their own policies. Requests can reveal which download or resource was requested.

Bridgecoil does not automatically attach the TV's app inventory, personal files, diagnostic logs, operation history or ADB private keys to these download requests. Optional TV helpers and other installed apps have their own behavior and policies.

Provider information: GitHub privacy statement.

Apple TestFlight and feedback

During TestFlight testing, Apple automatically collects crash logs and usage information and shares them with the developer. Testers cannot opt out of that TestFlight collection. Shared information can include app and device details, installations, sessions and crashes; email-invited testers' names and email addresses may accompany it. This information can be used to investigate beta issues and improve the app. Apple operates these services under its TestFlight and privacy terms.

If you send beta feedback, screenshots, logs or email to the developer, the information you choose to send can be used to investigate and respond to the issue. Review it first and remove pairing codes, private keys, credentials and personal file contents. Public GitHub issues are visible to others.

Platform information: TestFlight & Privacy.

Retention and deletion

Use app controls, where provided, to remove remembered devices or clear operation history. Removing the app or its data removes its local application storage according to the operating system's behavior. Manage device or cloud backups separately.

Exported files remain wherever you saved or shared them, including Files providers or cloud storage, until you remove them there. Forgetting a saved TV does not necessarily revoke ADB authorization on the TV; use the TV's debugging settings to revoke that trust.

Feedback you send may be retained while an issue is investigated or maintained in the service where it was submitted. Contact the developer about a private feedback or deletion request.

Privacy questions

For private beta questions or feedback-related requests, use TestFlight's developer feedback facility. For general project questions, use Bridgecoil's issue tracker without posting private device data.
